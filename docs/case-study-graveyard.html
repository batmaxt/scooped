Scooped · Competitive Case Study

The Ice Cream App Graveyard

Two apps already tried to be "the app for ice cream." Both are dead. Several adjacent apps — beer, wine, restaurant dishes, tinned fish — are alive and thriving. The difference between the two groups is a short list of specific mistakes, and Scooped is designed around every one of them.

Research conducted August 2026 · Untappd, Vivino, Beli, Letterboxd, Tinwise, and the autopsies of Dipt & Dippd

🪦 The graveyard

Every one of these had users, press, and a plausible pitch. Cause of death, in each case, was structural — not bad luck.

Dipt ICE CREAM · DEFUNCT
Cause of death: paywalled supply + orphaned flavors

The closest thing Scooped has to a direct predecessor. Dipt asked shops to pay before the app had an audience — so shops didn't join, so the app had no inventory, so users had no reason to open it. Its flavor data, where it existed, wasn't connected to live shop menus: flavors floated free of the one question that matters (can I get this near me right now?). It also launched off-season and never caught a summer wave.

Scooped's answer: menu upkeep is free for shops forever — fresh data can never sit behind a wallet. Every flavor in the catalog exists as availability at a shop, with a freshness state. And we launch on opening day.

Dippd ICE CREAM · DEFUNCT
Cause of death: off-season launch, no data flywheel

Same category, same fate. Launched when nobody was eating ice cream, burned its novelty press cycle into an empty winter, and had no mechanism for users to generate the data the app needed to be useful.

Scooped's answer: the April/May 2027 launch date isn't a guess — it's this autopsy. Build and seed all winter, go public riding the reopening wave, with "opening day" alerts as the launch feature.

Delectable WINE · FADED
Cause of death: scan match-rate rot

A wine-scanning darling that decayed when its label-recognition stopped keeping up. The moment scans stopped matching, contribution stopped, and the database froze in time.

Scooped's answer: match rate is the product. Menu scans that fail still grow the catalog, and the scanner tells the user so — every failed scan makes the next one work.

Foodspotting & Forkly DISHES · ACQUIRED / SHUT
Cause of death: undifferentiated from Instagram & Yelp

Photo-first dish apps with nothing a general platform couldn't copy. When Instagram became the default place to post food, there was no reason left to open them.

Scooped's answer: the moat is live board freshness — knowing what's in the case today. Instagram and Yelp structurally cannot do this; it requires a purpose-built contribution loop.

🍺 The living

The apps that work all found a durable loop. We studied each one for what to take — and what to refuse.

UntappdBeer · 4.9★

The category king, and still its #1 complaint is stale venue menus — staleness Untappd hides behind an invisible 15-day expiry. It charges venues $899+/yr and eats the stale-data complaints that pricing produces. Its bell-on-beer alerts and skip-signup browsing are proven retention machinery.

Take: alerts as the retention engine; registration-free browsing; freshness as the visible, named centerpiece Untappd never built. Refuse: charging shops for data upkeep; hiding staleness from users.

VivinoWine

The scan flywheel done right: a failed scan isn't an error, it's a contribution — and the app says so, promising every future scan of that label will work instantly.

Take: celebrate every scan as a contribution (Scooped's "You're the first to map this shop 🏆" moment).

BeliRestaurants

Its head-to-head ranking mechanic is beloved; its invite-wall is its most-hated pattern and a growth ceiling it built for itself.

Take: head-to-head duels — but same-flavor-across-shops ("Mint Chip at Coyle's vs. Van Leeuwen"), an apples-to-apples matchup Beli structurally can't offer. Refuse: gating anything behind invites, ever.

LetterboxdFilm

Proof that a logging app thrives on solo joy: lists, stats, a year-in-review — pride in small numbers, no friend graph required.

Take: design for the solo collector first. Social proof comes from geography (city leaderboards, rating histograms), not follower counts.

TinwiseTinned fish

A tiny-niche logging app that punches above its weight on pure charm — naming, voice, and delight standing in for scale.

Take: charm is strategy while small. Name everything (Taste Passport, Scout bounties, the weird menu's "maybe not that weird"). Utilitarian voice is for 74-million-user apps.

The nine rulings

What the study settled — adopted as product doctrine in August 2026. Numbered because they were decided in this order, and several are already shipped.

  1. Freshness is the wedge.A visible three-state ladder — Confirmed today / This week / Needs a scout — never raw timestamps. Stale shops become scout bounties: data decay is an engagement loop, not a bug.
  2. Alerts are the retention engine.Name-agnostic by design: an alert on "lemon custard" fires for any shop, any brand — including one you've never heard of. That surprise is the product's novelty.
  3. Minute one builds taste identity, never a network.Four-favorites onboarding (shipped, weird mode included). No invite gates, ever. Earned unlocks are fine.
  4. Every scan is a contribution — say so.Failed scans grow the catalog; the first person to map a shop gets celebrated by name.
  5. Design for the solo collector.Lists, streaks, stats, a shareable Summer in Review. Small numbers as pride.
  6. Flavor duels are the signature mechanic.Same flavor, two shops, pick a winner (shipped). Structurally impossible for every competitor studied.
  7. Never charge for inputs.Menu upkeep is free for shops forever. Charge for analytics, promotion, and signage extras — outputs, not inputs.
  8. Registration-free browsing.Anyone can see what's scooping nearby and set one alert before signing up. Only check-ins are gated.
  9. Charm is strategy while small.Every feature gets a name and a voice. Personality is a moat clones can't copy from a feature list.

Why the launch date is the strategy

Both dead ice cream apps launched into winter. Demand in this category isn't a curve you can push against — it's a wave you catch or miss.
